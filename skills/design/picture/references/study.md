# Imagery

The study covers 4,983 well-made sites: 996 technical, 982 humanistic and 3,005 expressive. All numbers below come from the counts across every site. Particulars such as sizes, treatments and placements come from notes on 900 of them. These are websites. The technical sites are mostly the marketing pages of technical products, not the products' own screens, and that difference matters in section 4.

## 1. What holds across characters

### A site carries pictures, and carries them throughout

- 4,657 of 4,983 sites (93.5%) have imagery. Only 326 (6.5%) are type alone.
- The usual role is **throughout**: 3,525 sites (71%) carry pictures from section to section. 773 (16%) use one hero picture and then go to type. 357 (7%) use imagery only as decoration.
- Imagery is part of the page's structure. A site with pictures in one section and none in the next reads as unfinished. Either carry imagery through, or give the hero one strong picture and let type carry the rest.

### Photography is the default kind

- Photo appears on 3,187 sites (64%), far ahead of the rest. Then come product UI on 1,183 (24%), abstract shapes on 869 (17%), video stills on 704 (14%), 3D on 702 (14%), hand-drawn illustration on 634 (13%), flat illustration on 609 (12%) and pattern on 488 (10%).
- Without a reason to do otherwise, use a real photograph of the product, the place, the people or the work. Pick illustration because the subject can't be photographed or because the brand draws, never as a generic filler.

### One art direction per set

- A grid of images reads as one family when every image follows one template. In the notes this shows up as:
  - blog covers sharing one look: a black field, a spotlight, a tag pill and a serif title;
  - article cards that all put a monochrome cut-out over a flat blue field;
  - thumbnails that all set a white serif title on a grainy blue gradient.
- Mixing photo styles, crops or treatments inside one set breaks this. Same crop ratio, same corner, same treatment.

### Pictures take the page's palette, or give it one

- Two moves recur, and both make the image and the interface one system:
  - **Treat the image to the palette.** Photos are duotoned into the brand ink: red on cream, mint, cobalt, plum on pink. Customer logos and showcase screenshots are set in greyscale so the page keeps one accent.
  - **Take the palette from the image.** The page background matches the product: a red device on a red field, a yellow console on yellow, teal lifted from a label. Or the interface stays black, cream and grey, so all the colour comes from the photographs or the packaging.
- Neutral chrome plus colourful imagery is the common case. Do not add an accent that competes with the pictures.

### Images cross edges

- The picture is often what joins two bands. Recurring forms in the notes:
  - a screenshot overhangs the bottom of the hero into the next section;
  - a product photo straddles the seam between a colour band and white;
  - a cut-out breaks out of the top of its card;
  - a UI crop bleeds off the bottom of its tile;
  - a carousel bleeds off the right edge to show there is more.
- Let one image per section do this on purpose. Do not box every picture inside its own bordered card.

### Captions and labels only when they add information

- Principle 1 applies to pictures. A caption that names what the image shows is noise.
- Well-made sites caption with what the picture cannot say: a place, a date, a credit, a client, a price.
- Portfolios usually show the work with no captions at all. The notes describe "no captions or labels on the tiles; the images alone carry the portfolio" on several sites.

### Pictures must stay legible and loaded

- The defects the notes record are concrete:
  - white text on a busy photo with no scrim;
  - dark navigation over a dark hero;
  - a hero washed almost to white to rescue its headline;
  - broken image boxes;
  - whole sections blank because lazy content never drew.
- When text sits on a photo, darken or tint the photo under it, or move the text off it. Load every image the design depends on.

## 2. Per character

### Technical: the product is the picture

Counts (996 sites): product UI 625 (63%), photo 407 (41%), abstract shapes 297 (30%), 3D 177 (18%), flat 142 (14%), pattern 136 (14%), video 118 (12%), hand-drawn 109 (11%), none 58 (6%). Role: throughout 760 (76%), decorative 90 (9%), hero only 87 (9%).

- **Show the real interface, staged.** The screenshot rarely floats on white. It sits on one of:
  - a soft pastel or grainy mesh gradient;
  - an offset flat colour slab that peeks out behind it;
  - a painterly landscape or oil painting (five of the notes);
  - a dark glow, the "lit from behind" violet or blue horizon.
- **Fade or crop the screenshot; do not frame it in heavy chrome.** It fades into the page at its bottom edge, or turns to grey skeleton bars so that one highlighted element stands out.
- **Crop to the moment.** Feature tiles show a fragment: a status pill, a cursor on a button, an alert chip. They do not show the whole app.
- **Place the product in life with floating UI over photos.** A frosted card or a balance chip laid over a lifestyle photo shows the product in use without a full screenshot.
- **Abstract shapes are the second language at 30%.** They are glows, orbit rings, blueprint grids and dot fields. Use one, behind the hero. Do not scatter them.
- **Pricing, help and contact pages go quiet.** About 25 of these pages are in the notes. About 18 have no imagery, or one decorative touch: a pale glow, a small icon per plan, an ink drawing. The decorative 9% lives here.
- **Ink-line spot illustrations** (black line, one spot colour) recur on technical blogs, pricing and support pages, where there is no UI to show.

### Humanistic: photographs of real life, throughout

Counts (982 sites): photo 789 (80%), product UI 201 (20%), hand-drawn 162 (16.5%), flat 134 (14%), video 112 (11%), abstract shapes 106 (11%), 3D 87 (9%), pattern 66 (7%), none 27 (3%). Role: throughout 808 (82%), hero only 109 (11%), decorative 38 (4%).

- **This is the most pictured character.** It has the highest photo share and the highest "throughout" share. Only 27 sites are type alone.
- **Warm, natural light.** "Warm, natural-light lifestyle" is the phrase the notes repeat most: people, hands, rooms, food, with film grain allowed. Cool stock photography is rare.
- **Products as cut-outs on a matched ground.** The product is shot on the page's own tint, or set on pale grey tiles, with no card frame. The item floats and the grid reads as one surface.
- **Soft frames.** Rounded corners, arches, pills, circles, a matted border like a print, a photo slightly rotated like a snapshot. Choose one frame shape and repeat it.
- **Hand-drawn marks are more common here than flat illustration** (162 against 134). They appear as ink doodles, an underline, an arrow, a mascot beside a photo. They accompany photography rather than replace it.

**Games** (about 17 sites in the humanistic notes) differ sharply. Almost none use photography.
- The imagery is a full-bleed 3D or flat illustrated scene, often a single fixed title screen that does not scroll.
- The scene carries one play or begin button, a sound toggle and little else.
- Celebration lives in the scene's colour and motion, not in added badges.

**Restaurants and bars** (about 20 sites in the notes) share one look:
- dark, warm, low-key photography in amber and red;
- near-black or cream grounds, with the palette taken from the photos;
- photos set in inset or rounded frames with even gutters, for example about 16 px gutters and 18 px radius;
- the menu set as a typographic list, with hairline rows and prices aligned right, never as a picture.

### Expressive: the work, the product, or nothing

Counts (3,005 sites): photo 1,991 (66%), video 474 (16%), abstract shapes 466 (16%), 3D 438 (15%), hand-drawn 363 (12%), product UI 357 (12%), flat 333 (11%), pattern 286 (10%), none 240 (8%). Role: throughout 1,957 (65%), hero only 577 (19%), none 240 (8%), decorative 229 (8%).

- **This character uses the most hero-only pages (19%) and the most type-only pages (8%).** Expressive sites commit: one image that holds the screen, or no image at all.
- **Portfolios** are the largest group in the expressive notes. In them the work is the hero:
  - no headline, or a one-line bio in its place;
  - tiles edge to edge with 2–8 px gutters, in a masonry, a contact sheet or a sideways strip;
  - tiny mono captions, or none.
  - The chrome stays monochrome so the work supplies all the colour.
- **Video** is used more here than in any other character (16%). It is a full-bleed reel or footage behind a small headline set low.
- **Fixed scenes.** WebGL objects, 3D renders that overlap the wordmark, a product that sits in front of edge-to-edge type. The image and the headline are composed as one.
- **Editorial and magazine sites** (about 20 sites in the notes) use:
  - uniform crops (3:2 or square) with square corners;
  - a small uppercase kicker over a serif headline;
  - hairline rules between stories instead of cards.
- **Type-only pages** (240 sites) are legitimate. A giant wordmark, a list of names or a manifesto carries the screen. Do not add a stock picture to fill it.

## 3. Recipes

**A technical product hero.** Rests on product UI in 625 of 996 technical sites.
- A centred headline, with the full app screenshot below it, wider than the text column.
- The screenshot sits on a soft pastel gradient or a painted landscape. It fades into the page at its bottom edge, or overhangs the hero band into the next section.
- A greyscale customer-logo strip sits directly under it.
- No device bezel, and no caption naming the screen.

**A technical bento of cropped UI.**
- Light-grey tiles with a large radius (about 24–40 px).
- Each tile has a short title and one grey line at the top.
- A real UI fragment is pinned to the bottom of the tile and bleeds off its edge.
- One tile in the set may be dark for contrast.

**A humanistic shop.** Rests on photo in 789 of 982 humanistic sites.
- A 50/50 hero: a flat brand-colour text panel beside a full-height warm lifestyle photo that bleeds to the right edge.
- Below it, products as cut-outs on the page tint or on pale grey tiles, with no border. Name and price sit under each tile.
- One frame shape (arch, pill or rounded rectangle) repeats down the page.

**A restaurant or bar.** About 20 sites in the notes.
- Dark, warm, low-light photos, one per band, at near-full width.
- Rounded or inset frames with an even gutter of about 16 px.
- Strictly cream and near-black type, so all the colour comes from the photographs.
- The menu as a hairline-ruled list with prices aligned right.

**An expressive portfolio.** The largest expressive group in the notes.
- No hero headline.
- The work runs edge to edge with 2–8 px gutters, at mixed tile sizes or in a sideways strip.
- Each piece has either a tiny mono caption (client, year) or nothing.
- The navigation is three or four small words at the screen's corners.
- The interface stays black, white and grey.

**A game title screen.** About 17 humanistic sites in the notes.
- One fixed full-bleed scene, in 3D or flat illustration, darkened under the title.
- A wordmark, one play or begin button, and a sound toggle in a corner.
- Nothing scrolls, and there is no navigation bar.

## Before you finish

| You see | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| An expressive or humanistic page with no image where the brand has products, places or people | A real photograph, carried through the page |
| A technical tool's own screens with illustrations | Remove them; imagery belongs on the product's site, not in the tool |
| A technical landing page with stock photos and no product | The product's real UI, staged on a soft field and cropped to the moment |
| A full screenshot in a heavy device frame, on white | Drop the frame; set it on a gradient or slab and fade or crop it |
| Images in one set with mixed crops, corners or treatments | One art direction: same ratio, frame and treatment |
| An accent colour fighting colourful photography | Neutral chrome; let the pictures carry colour, or tone them to the ink |
| A caption naming what the picture shows | Remove it; caption only a place, date, credit or client |
| Glows, blobs and patterns behind several sections | One, behind the hero, or none |
| Text over a busy photo with no scrim | Darken or tint the photo under the text, or move the text |
| A typographic expressive page padded with a stock image | Remove it; type alone is the composition |
| A game page built from photos and a scrolling feature list | One full-bleed scene, one play button, a sound toggle |
