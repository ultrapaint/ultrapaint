# Profiles

How the studied library builds it, counted over the pieces of its kind in a study of 575 (a share is of that kind's 23 pieces; "~n notes" is at least n). Where it differs from the design skills, the skills win: they hold what a person preferred when the two were compared. These patterns are common in the study, which does not make them right.

- A cover band with the avatar overlapping its bottom edge and a verified badge on the avatar (~11 notes). Some covers use gradients and holographic seals. On a technical product, leave those out.
- Stats are a **small muted label over a large number**, with thin vertical rules between the cells (~7). Contact details use the same label-over-value grid (~10 across kinds). Use the grid when the values are the headline.
- The name is bold and the line under it is muted, as in nearly every identity cell in the study.
- Dotted rules separate the blocks inside the card (~7). Where space already holds the blocks apart, the rule is noise.
- Read-only values are plain text with an Edit link at the row's end, laid out as label | value | action (~3, with ~9 more in settings). Beside the photo, the delete action is muted and comes before the accent upload (~3).
- 43% of pieces put actions in the header. Either emphasis rises toward the right (kebab, then outline, then filled) (~3), or the buttons are equal outlines with leading icons (~3). The skill allows an icon on at most one header button.
- Fields are labelled in 93% of pieces and carry helper text in 20%.
- 48% of pieces put an icon on their labels. An icon beside every label is decoration.
- 59% of headers carry a description, with a median of 6 words. Keep it only if it says something the title does not.
- Team pages (~7) give every portrait one treatment: duotone, halftone, or greyscale with colour on hover (5). They use no cards (~5). A serif on team members' roles is one of the ~4 serifs found in product screens; interface text stays sans.
