# Typography: what well-made sites do

This reference rests on 4,983 sites chosen by people: 996 technical, 982 humanistic and 3,005 expressive. Numbers come from the counts of all 4,983 sites. Particulars come from notes on 900 of them, about 300 per character. Most of these sites are public pages: landing pages, shops, portfolios and magazines. Their numbers set the hero and the page. The typography skill's interface rules (labels, fields, navigation, floors) are not tested here and still stand.

Expressive sites are 60% of the whole, so the "all sites" numbers lean expressive. Read the per-character numbers before applying a default.

---

## 1. What holds across characters

### Set text in a sans, and load the face
- **Body text is a sans on 3,988 of 4,983 sites (80%).**
  - Neo-grotesque leads with 2,635 (53%).
  - Geometric follows with 913 (18%).
- **Headings are more varied:**
  - neo-grotesque 1,489 (30%)
  - serif display 1,252 (25%)
  - geometric 726 (15%)
  - grotesque display 534 (11%)
  - condensed 381 (8%)
- **Name the face and load it.** Inter is the most common face, on 494 sites (10%).
- **Fallback faces are rare, and almost always a deliberate brutalist choice:** Arial 57, Helvetica 57, Times New Roman 35. Where a fallback is accidental, the notes call it a defect:
  - a fallback serif in a calculator's wordmark and labels "clashes with the sans UI around it";
  - a default-serif legal footer "is left unstyled against the designed page".

### Give the page one large headline, about 62 px
- The median hero heading is **62 px** (n = 4,961): technical 62, humanistic 60, expressive 64.
- Everything else on the page is smaller. The headline carries the rank, not a stack of large lines.

### Tighten display type
- **Headings are set tight on 2,900 sites (58%), normal on 1,683 (34%) and wide on 400 (8%).**
- Wide tracking belongs to small capitals and spaced wordmarks, not to running headlines.

### Let size carry the headline, not weight
- **Regular is the most common display weight:** 2,084 sites (42%).
  - bold 1,208 (24%)
  - medium 714 (14%)
  - light 509 (10%)
  - black 468 (9%)
- A large headline does not need bold. Most well-made sites rank with size and leave the weight at 400 or 500.

### Write headlines in sentence case
- **Sentence case: 2,423 sites (49%).** Title case: 1,280. Uppercase: 1,280.
- Uppercase is a character choice (section 2), not a default.

### Use about five sizes on a page
- The median page uses **5 text sizes** (n = 4,960): technical 6, humanistic 6, expressive 5.
- This counts a whole scrolling page. A single screen of a product still keeps to the skill's three or four.

### Set apart one thing in a headline, or nothing
- **2,782 sites (56%) set no accent in the headline at all.**
- The other 2,201 set apart one thing, in one of these ways:
  - a muted second line;
  - one word in a serif italic;
  - one word in the accent colour;
  - a highlighter bar.
- One device per headline. The notes that stack several (gradient, sticker, emoji and outline at once) are brand pages, and none of them is quiet.

### Keep two text colours plainly apart
The well-made version, from the notes:
- a near-black statement followed by a grey continuation;
- a white title over a dim grey subhead;
- "the subhead is tiny and dim grey under a large white headline, so the size and contrast gap does all the ranking".

The failures, also from the notes:
- "dark nav text over a dark hero photo is nearly unreadable";
- "light grey headings on charcoal panels give low contrast";
- "white headline on a busy photo with no scrim costs legibility".

### Technical: one neo-grotesque, tight, sentence case

**The face**
- Use Inter, or a face like it. Inter alone covers 301 of 996 sites (30%), and Inter Display a further 28.
- A geometric sans is the second choice: 198 headings (20%).
- Geist is in the counts but rare: 6 sites, plus 5 for Geist Mono.

**Display settings**
- Set the display tight. 812 of 996 sites (82%) do; only 10 track wide.
- Use sentence case. 789 sites (79%) do; uppercase headlines are 44 (4%).
- Set the headline at regular or medium weight: 610 of 996 (61%). Bold follows at 263 (26%).

**Mono belongs to labels and code, not to headings or body**
- Mono headings are 18 (2%) and mono body 30 (3%).
- In the notes, mono carries the small things:
  - eyebrows: "▶ CASE STUDIES", "[ 01 / 09 ]", "/ PASSWORDLESS";
  - button labels and stat captions;
  - code and ids: terminal commands, a "0:00:00" timer, "ap-southeast-2".
- Uppercase and wide tracking go with it, at 10–12 px.

**A serif display on the landing page only**
- 171 technical sites (17%) put a serif display on the landing page, most often a paid editorial serif.
- In every case the product screenshots beneath stay sans. Section 3 has the recipe.

**The common accent is a muted continuation**
- The second sentence or clause of the headline drops to the muted grey. More than 30 of the 300 technical notes do this.
- Examples: a product's claim with its promise in grey on the next line, and "the first clause is black and the continuation is grey".
- It uses the screen's own second colour, so it adds no new colour.

### Humanistic: a serif or friendly display over a plain sans body

**Faces**
- Serif display is the first heading face here: 335 of 982 (34%), and with text serifs 372 (38%).
  - Named in the counts: Canela 13, Domaine Display 10, Reckless 8, GT Alpina 8, Windsor 8.
- Geometric headings follow at 178 (18%).
- Humanist sans headings are rare: 32 (3%).
- The body stays sans: neo-grotesque 415 (42%) and geometric 286 (29%). Poppins (19) and DM Sans (17) follow Inter (76).

**Settings**
- Expect more uppercase and more open tracking than technical.
  - Uppercase headlines: 211 (21%).
  - Wide tracking: 91 (9%), nine times the technical share.
  - Normal tracking: 377 (38%).
- Bold is a real second choice: 278 headings (28%).

**Restaurants and bars.** About two dozen in the notes, and they differ from the rest. Set the page like a printed menu:
- uppercase headings, either a tall condensed face or a wide-tracked serif;
- address, hours and phone as centred stacks of letterspaced small caps;
- hairline rules between sections;
- dish names in a tracked face, with prices right-aligned;
- dot leaders for opening hours;
- one or two ink colours on a tinted ground: a single deep teal, cream on ochre, gold on black.

**Games.** About fifteen in the notes. The title is a lockup, often drawn rather than set:
- pixel or bitmap letters, extruded 3D letters, swashed serifs;
- under it, one large start button;
- menu items as small pixel or mono words, "styled like a console menu".

Keep the game's own interface text in the product's sans.

### Expressive: the widest range, set by the brand

**Faces**
- Heading faces split almost evenly:
  - neo-grotesque 875 (29%)
  - serif display 746 (25%)
  - geometric 350 (12%)
  - grotesque display 332 (11%)
  - condensed 286 (10%)
- No face dominates. Inter reaches only 117 (4%), next to Neue Haas Grotesk 63, Helvetica Neue 54, GT America 40, Graphik 39, Founders Grotesk 39 and Apercu 38.

**Settings**
- Uppercase is common: 1,025 headings (34%), nearly level with sentence case at 1,126 (37%).
- Regular weight leads further than anywhere else: 1,364 (45%).
- Scale goes to extremes in the notes:
  - wordmarks set edge to edge at 150–480 px;
  - portfolios set entirely at one size of 13–15 px, "hierarchy comes only from position and whitespace".
- The median stays 64 px and 5 sizes.

**Editorial magazines and news.** About twenty in the notes, and they differ:
- serif headlines at 34–50 px;
- a small uppercase kicker in sans or mono, often in one accent colour, above each headline;
- tracked uppercase bylines after an italic "By";
- hairline rules between stories instead of cards;
- serif reading text in many of them. This is where most of the expressive serif bodies (458) sit.

**Studio, designer and artist portfolios.** Dozens in the notes. They pick one of two poles, and rarely sit between:
- a giant name with tiny corner labels; or
- one small size for everything.

## 3. Recipes

### Technical product hero
Rests on 996 technical sites; the muted continuation on 30+ of the 300 technical notes.

- **Face:** Inter or Inter Display (329 of 996).
- **Headline:** 56–80 px (median 62), medium or regular (61%), tracking −0.02em to −0.03em (82% tight), sentence case (79%).
- **Two lines:** the second sentence in the muted grey, same size and weight.
- **Subhead:** one 16–18 px line in muted grey.
- **Actions:** a filled button beside a grey-filled or ghost one, both labels at the same medium weight.
- **Small labels:** uppercase mono, 11–12 px, tracked 0.05em.
- **Eyebrow:** keep a small pill above the headline only if it carries news, such as a release, a date or a version.

### Editorial serif on a technical product
Rests on 171 technical sites (17%); in the notes, AI, developer, payroll, recruiting and marketing products.

- **Headline:** a serif display at 56–88 px, regular weight, tight, sentence case. Use a free one (Instrument Serif, Newsreader, Source Serif 4, Fraunces); the paid editorial serifs these sites use need a licence the user holds (`references/design-system/foundations.md`).
- **Body:** a small grey sans, usually Inter.
- **Screenshots:** the product UI keeps its sans. "The app UI itself uses the same serif for note titles" (an AI notes app) is the one exception: content titles, never controls.
- **Accent, if any:** one word in the same serif's italic.

### Consumer brand page: shop, clinic, hotel, food brand
Rests on 982 humanistic sites: serif display 335, sans body 701 (71%).

- **Headline:** a serif display at 44–80 px, regular or light, tight. The second line or one key word may switch to the italic of the same serif.
- **Body:** a neo-grotesque or geometric sans at 16 px.
- **Small text:** buttons, eyebrows and product meta in 11–12 px uppercase with wide tracking (0.1em and up).
- **Layout:** sections separated by bands of colour, not by more type.

### Restaurant as a printed menu
Rests on about two dozen restaurant and bar notes; humanistic uppercase 211 and wide tracking 91 in the counts.

- **Headlines:** uppercase, a condensed sans at 56–110 px or a serif at 40–60 px tracked 0.1em.
- **Practical details:** hours and address in 12–13 px letterspaced small caps, centred.
- **Menu:** dish name left and price right-aligned on one row; hairline rules between groups; dot leaders on the hours table.
- **Colour:** one ink on a warm ground.

### Wordmark hero for a portfolio, studio or product launch
Rests on 3,005 expressive sites (uppercase 1,025, regular 1,364), with dozens of notes.

- **Wordmark:** the name or product set edge to edge at 150–400 px, tracked tight, regular or bold, uppercase or title case. Often a product render or photo overlaps the letters.
- **Everything else:** 11–14 px labels pinned to the corners: role, city, a live local clock, contact.
- **Sizes:** two to four in total, with nothing between giant and tiny.

### Editorial front page
Rests on about twenty magazine and news notes, within expressive serif-text bodies 409.

- **Lead headline:** serif at 40–50 px; secondary headlines at 24–34 px.
- **Kicker:** 11 px uppercase sans or mono in one accent colour above each headline.
- **Byline:** 11 px tracked uppercase grey.
- **Dek:** serif, muted.
- **Structure:** hairline rules, not boxes. Columns divided by vertical hairlines.
