# Components: what well-made sites do

This reference rests on a study of 4,983 sites chosen by people: 996 technical, 982 humanistic and 3,005 expressive. Numbers come from all of them. Particulars come from notes on 900 of them, up to 300 per character.

Most of these are websites: landing pages, shops, portfolios, magazines. Even the "technical" ones are mostly the marketing sites of developer and SaaS products, not the tools themselves. Read the numbers as how a product presents itself. The components skill still governs the product's own screens.

Expressive sites are 60% of the total, so the all-sites figures lean their way. When a default differs by character, use the character's own figure.

---

## 1. What holds across characters

### Most content needs no card
- 55% of all sites (2,752 of 4,983) use no cards at all.
- When a group does need a surface, a **fill** one step off the page is the first choice: 25% (1,249).
- A **border** comes second: 15% (749).
- A **shadow** comes last: 5% (231).

The order fill > border > shadow holds in every character:

| Character | Fill | Border | Shadow |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | 40% | 34% | 10% |
| Humanistic | 34% | 14% | 7% |
| Expressive | 17% | 9% | 2% |

So a group is held by a quiet fill before a line, and by a line before a shadow. When a screen does several jobs, give each job its own surface. When it does one job, give it none.

### Radius sits at 12–16 px
- The median radius is **12 px** across the 2,792 sites with a recorded radius.
- Technical sites: **12 px** (n = 916).
- Humanistic and expressive sites: **16 px** (n = 708 and n = 1,168).

Pick one radius per family and keep it. In the notes, a site's buttons, cards and inputs share it. When buttons are pills, they are pills everywhere.

### One primary action, and a quieter second
- An outline button is rarely the whole system. Outline is the only button style on 11% of sites, and 5–12% within each character.
- The common pattern is a filled primary beside something quieter. In the notes the quieter partner is one of:
  - a light grey fill of the same height,
  - a white button with a hairline border,
  - a plain text link with a chevron.
- Make the second action quieter than the first. Do not make it a second colour.
- Repeat the same primary style wherever the action recurs.

### Navigation is a bar or almost nothing
- Top bar or minimal navigation covers 79% of sites (top bar 2,412, minimal 1,545).
- Within each character: technical 94%, humanistic 84%, expressive 73%.
- A hamburger on desktop is 6% (301).
- A sidebar is 2% (115).

On a website, do not hide the navigation or move it to a rail without a reason the brief gives.

### Groups by rule, not by box
The notes return to the same quiet devices again and again:
- Hairline rules between list rows.
- One bordered container split by 1 px dividers, instead of separate boxed cards.
- Bands of background colour between sections, instead of dividers.

These are structure. Use them before adding a heading or a box.

### Two text levels
Across characters, the notes keep a near-black lead and a visibly lighter grey for the rest:
- a headline's first line dark and its continuation grey;
- meta lines in grey under a dark title;
- a label's value dark and the label grey.

This is principle 8 in practice. It works because the grey is plainly lighter, not a near-black.

## 2. Per character

### Technical (n = 996)
- **Buttons:** rounded 44%, pill 40%, square 13%, text-link 4%. Style is mixed on 63%: a filled primary beside a lighter secondary. Filled-only is 28%. Ghost or outline-only is 9%.
- **Cards:** fill 40%, border 34%, none 16%, shadow 10%.
- **Navigation:** top bar 82%, minimal 11%. A hamburger (1%) or a sidebar (1%) almost never appears.
- **Radius:** median 12 px.

Particulars from the notes:
- Feature grids sit in one hairline-bordered box whose cells share 1 px dividers.
- Bento tiles are a near-page fill with the title and a grey one-liner at top left, and a crop of real UI bleeding off the bottom edge.
- Logo walls are greyscale and come right under the hero.
- Labels, eyebrows and figures are set in a small uppercase mono.
- Pricing uses a monthly/yearly segmented control with a small savings note beside it.

Keep the product's own screens to the components skill. This marketing register belongs to the product's site, not its tables.

### Humanistic (n = 982)
- **Buttons:** pill 40%, square 26%, rounded 24%, text-link 10%. Style is filled on 46%, the highest of any character. Mixed is 31%.
- **Cards:** none 44%, fill 34%, border 14%, shadow 7%. A soft fill is chosen over a line by more than 2 to 1.
- **Navigation:** top bar 67%, minimal 17%, none 11%.
- **Radius:** median 16 px.

Particulars from the notes:
- Pages sit on warm grounds: cream, off-white, sage, peach.
- Sections are rounded panels inset 8–16 px from the viewport.
- Social proof goes directly under the call to action: stars, an avatar stack, press logos.

Two finer genres differ enough to name:

- **Shops, restaurants, hotels and bars** (dozens of notes) run **square, 0 px** buttons with tiny, widely tracked uppercase labels. Outlines are hairline. A serif carries the display headings. This is where the 26% square comes from.
  - Product tiles have no chrome: the image on a light grey fill, then the name and the price.
  - A badge ("New", "Bestseller") sits in one corner of the image.
  - A struck-through old price sits beside the sale price.
- **Games** (about fifteen notes) are a single fixed screen: a title lockup, one large button, and a sound toggle as a small control in a corner. Nothing scrolls until the player starts.

### Expressive (n = 3,005)
- **Buttons:** text-link 53%, pill 22%, square 18%, rounded 7%. Style is ghost on 53%. More than half of expressive sites have no filled button at all. Their actions are words: an underlined link, "↳ Browse all work", "[ SEE ALL ]", "More ↓".
- **Cards:** none on 72%. Fill 17%, border 9%, shadow 2%.
- **Navigation:** minimal 42% leads the top bar at 31%. None is 15%, hamburger 8%, sidebar 3%. Minimal means links pinned to the corners or spread across the width, a single comma-separated line, or a short stacked list.
- **Radius:** median 16 px. Many sites run 0 px throughout.

Particulars from the notes:
- Hierarchy comes from position and scale, often with only two type sizes.
- Lists of work are tables with hairline rows: year, title, client in grey.
- Two finer genres:
  - **Portfolios** let the work stand alone, without captions beyond a client name and a year.
  - **Editorial and magazine** sites separate stories with hairline rules instead of boxes. A tiny kicker sits above a serif headline, with a small grey byline under it.

## 3. Recipes

### A technical product's hero
- **Eyebrow (optional):** one pill announcement chip only when there is news: a small "New" tag, one line, an arrow.
- **Headline:** two tones, a near-black statement and a grey continuation at the same size.
- **Buttons:** a black filled pill or a rounded 8 px button, beside a light grey filled button of equal height or a text link with a chevron.
- **Proof:** a greyscale logo row directly below, with no heading over it.
- **Product:** shown as a real screenshot, cropped and fading into the page.
- **Frame:** a top-bar nav (82% of technical sites) and a 12 px radius on everything.

### A feature grid without cards
- **Container:** one container with a 1 px border, split into a 2- or 3-column grid by shared hairline dividers.
- **Cell content:**
  - a bold title and two grey lines;
  - optionally a crop of real UI pinned to the cell's bottom edge.
- **What to leave out:** no icon on every cell and no box per cell.
- **For a bento instead:** tiles filled one step off the page at 16–24 px radius, with the UI crop bleeding off the bottom edge. This is the 40% fill of technical sites.

### A pricing page
- **Plans:** three or four in one row.
- **Billing:** a monthly/yearly segmented control above the plans, with the saving in one small note beside it.
- **Recommended plan:** mark it **once**, either with a tab on its top edge or as the only filled button among outline buttons. The notes show sites marking it three ways at once: a border, a tab and a unique colour. One is enough.
- **Prices:** large, with the unit small and grey beside them.
- **Comparison table:** hairline rows, a filled check where a plan has the feature and a faint dash where it does not.

### A shop or a table to book
- **Buttons:** square, 0 px, with tiny uppercase labels tracked about 0.1em.
  - One filled button: dark on cream, or white on a photo.
  - Every other action is a hairline outline.
- **Product tile:** an image on a light grey fill, then the name and the price on one row. No border and no shadow.
- **Badge:** one, in the image's top-left corner.
- **Type:** a serif for the display headings, a sans for everything a shopper reads to act.
- **Navigation:** split around a centred wordmark.
- **What to leave out:** an email modal over the hero on load. The notes show many, and they bury the page.

### A portfolio or studio index
- **Actions:** no buttons. Navigation is a few text links at the corners or spread across the top. The current page is marked by an underline or an outline pill.
- **Work list:**
  - Either rows split by hairlines: year in grey, title in near-black, client in grey, an arrow aligned right.
  - Or a gutterless image grid with no captions.
- **Type:** two sizes at most.
- **Colour:** comes from the work alone.

### An editorial front page
- **Grid:** three columns of unequal width: a lead story, a narrow list, and a column led by images.
- **Separators:** columns and stories are split by hairline rules, never by cards.
- **Each story:**
  - a tiny kicker in uppercase mono or sans;
  - a serif headline;
  - a grey standfirst;
  - a small byline.
- **Section titles:** small, set between rules.
