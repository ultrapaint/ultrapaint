# Colour: what well-made sites do

This reference comes from a study of 4,983 websites that people picked as well made: 996 technical, 982 humanistic and 3,005 expressive. Every number comes from the counts for all 4,983 sites. The particulars (which element takes the accent, which hue, what fails) come from notes on 900 of them. Read it with the colour and character skills. Where the sites and the principles disagree, the principles win.

**What the sample is.** Most "technical" sites here are the *marketing* pages of technical products: landing pages, pricing, blogs, help centres. They are not the admin screens themselves. For a tool's own interface, the colour skill still governs. These counts tell you how its public pages are coloured.

---

## 1. What holds across characters

### Surfaces are light, and the light is often tinted
- 3,075 of 4,983 sites (61.7%) sit on a light ground: light-tint 1,569 (31.5%) and white 1,506 (30.2%).
- Light-tint is the single most common background. In the notes it is mostly warm: cream, warm off-white, beige, stone, "warm grey paper". Cool tints (pale mint, lavender-grey) are rarer.
- Dark grounds are a minority: black 523 (10.5%) and dark 456 (9.2%), together 19.6%.
- Default to a white or warm off-white surface.
- Keep neutrals neutral or warm, never blue-grey.

### Two hues, not five
- The median palette is **2 hues** beyond the neutrals. This holds overall (n = 4,096 sites with a count) and in every character.
- Notes with a palette of 0 are common among portfolios and galleries. Palettes of 6 or more are rare and almost always category systems: one hue per product, per flavour or per section.
- Pick one accent.
- Add a second hue only with its own fixed job: categories, a promotion, data.

### Contrast is high
- Text contrast is high on 3,642 sites (73.1%), medium on 1,220 (24.5%) and low on 119 (2.4%).
- In the notes, the low cases are flaws more often than choices:
  - "nav is thin white text over a bright photo with no scrim"
  - "amber italic text over the image gets hard to read"
  - "dark nav text over a dark hero photo is nearly unreadable"
- Compute every pair (see the colour skill). A medium-contrast body is a brand risk, not a default.

### Gradients are the exception
- 1,272 sites (25.5%) use a gradient; 3,709 do not.
- Where gradients appear in the notes, they are *light*: a glow behind a product screenshot, a sky wash in a hero, a band that fades into the next section.
- They are almost never a surface for interface chrome.

### Two text levels, and grey as emphasis
The notes keep finding one move: a heading set in two values.
- The claim is in the foreground: "a dark statement, then a grey continuation", "white claim, grey explanation".
- About 25 of the 300 technical notes use it, and it recurs in humanistic and expressive notes.

This is principle 8 at heading scale. The second level is the muted token, at the same size and weight, and nothing else changes. It works only when the two values are plainly apart.

### Colour comes from imagery as often as from the interface
- At least 143 sites, in the most frequent phrasings alone, describe their accent as "none": "colour comes only from photography", "strictly black on white", "colour comes only from the project images".
- Nearly all of them are expressive.
- When the content is photographs or work, keep the interface monochrome and let the content carry the hue.

## 2. Where the characters differ

### Technical: neutral ground, one accent, light behind the product
**Surfaces**
- Light grounds on 699 of 996 sites (70.2%), white slightly ahead of tint.
- Dark grounds on 232 (23.3%), the most of any character. Near-black (dark, 128) beats pure black (104).
- Image backgrounds are almost absent (15), and so are full colour floods (50).

**Contrast**
- The highest of all: 81.6% high, 0.5% low.
- The two-tone grey heading is a technical signature.

**Gradients**
- Nearly half (48.3%) use one. The notes say how:
  - a violet glow behind the top edge of the screenshot, "so the product looks lit from behind"
  - a sky gradient fading to the white page
  - a screenshot that "fades to black at the bottom, so the product dissolves into the page"
- The gradient lights the product; it does not decorate the chrome.

**Where the accent goes, from the notes**
- The primary call to action, as a filled pill.
- Text links.
- The active tab or filter.
- Small uppercase or mono eyebrows.
- Often, one key phrase in the headline.

**What stays neutral**
- Client logos are greyscale or monochrome, almost without exception: "showcase screenshots are desaturated so the page keeps one accent hue".
- Many primaries are solid near-black rather than a hue. In one note, "purple is kept for the product UI only".

**Pricing pages**
- About 15 notes.
- The recommended plan is told apart by one colour move:
  - the only dark card in the row
  - a tinted column running the full height of the comparison table
  - the only filled button among outlines
- Use one such move. Using all three at once is noise.

### Humanistic: warm tint, tinted ink, a livelier accent
**Surfaces**
- Light-tint leads (388, 39.5%): cream, sand, blush, sage.
- Pure black is rare (33, 3.4%). Warm products avoid it.
- Colour floods (125) and photo grounds (69) are about as common as in expressive sites.

**Ink**
- The foreground is often a deep brand hue instead of black: navy, aubergine, forest green, olive-brown, deep teal.
- "Headings and body text are dark olive-brown instead of black, which keeps them soft."

**Accent**
- Livelier and used more widely: buttons, stars, prices, category tags, whole bands.
- Category systems are common: "each content type has its own colour", "each flavour sits on a circular gradient in its own colours".

**Restaurants, bars and hotels (about 30 notes)**
- They differ. Most run on one ink and one ground: olive on cream, ochre with cream type, deep teal throughout, near-black with gold.
- Every other colour comes from the food and room photography.
- Contrast drifts to medium more than elsewhere. Compute it.

**Games (about 15 notes)**
- A dark or black ground with one glowing accent: cyan, teal, lime.
- The accent is used as light, not as a fill. Often one big filled button: "a big lime PLAY button laid over its centre".

### Expressive: monochrome or a single flood
**Surfaces**
- The most varied mix: white 28.4%, tint 27.9%, black 12.8%, colorful 12.7%, image 9.5%, dark 8.5%.
- Pure black (386) beats near-black (256). Here black is a stage, not a dark mode.

**Gradients**
- The fewest of any character (17.3%).

**Low contrast**
- 99 of the 119 low-contrast sites are expressive. Some are deliberate: pale grey text revealed word by word on scroll. Many are not.
- Do not treat low contrast as part of the character.

**Two strong patterns from the notes, and little in between**
- **No interface colour:** black, white and greys, with the work supplying all the hue. This is portfolios, studios and galleries, and almost all of the "none" accents.
- **One flood colour:** a whole page in signal red, butter yellow, neon green or cobalt, with type in black, white or the same hue tone-on-tone.

**Editorial and magazines (about 20 notes)**
- White or cream ground.
- One ink accent reserved for the masthead, kickers or bylines: coral, yellow, purple, crimson, orange.
- Photography supplies the rest.

## 3. Recipes

### Dark product hero (technical)
*Rests on 232 dark or black technical sites and 481 with gradients.*
- **Ground:** near-black.
- **Headline:** off-white, with its second line in the muted grey.
- **Accent:** one hue (violet, blue, lime), used as light: a soft glow behind the top edge of the product screenshot, the screenshot fading to the page at its bottom.
- **Buttons:**
  - Primary: a solid white pill, or the accent with its foreground pair.
  - Secondary: a dark grey fill or a plain text link with a chevron.
- **Logos:** greyscale.
- **Cards:** one step lighter than the page, told apart by tone or a 1px low-contrast border.
- No second hue except data in the product mockup.

### Warm-light product page (technical, humanistic)
*Rests on 699 light technical sites and 683 light humanistic sites.*
- **Ground:** warm off-white or cream. The foreground is near-black or a deep brand ink that computes to 15:1.
- **Accent:** one saturated hue on the primary call to action, on links and on the active tab.
- **Optional extras:**
  - A small accent eyebrow, only where it says what the heading does not.
  - One accent phrase in the hero headline.
- **Screenshot:** on a pale tint of the accent, or offset over a flat colour slab.
- **Logo strip:** monochrome.
- **Status in the mockup:** dots, not tinted blocks.

### One-ink brand page (humanistic, expressive)
*Rests on the 2-hue median and about 30 hospitality notes.*
- **Ground:** cream.
- **Ink:** one deep hue (forest green, navy, cobalt, terracotta) carries every heading, body line, rule and outline button.
- **Fill:** one filled button at most, in the same ink.
- **Hue:** photographs supply all other colour.
- **Contrast:** check the ink on cream. Single-ink pages slide to medium contrast. Darken the ink until it computes, rather than adding black.

### Flood colour (expressive, humanistic)
*Rests on 556 colorful sites: 381 expressive, 125 humanistic, 50 technical.*
- **Ground:** the whole page in one saturated hue, often the product's own colour ("the background changes … to a full saturated yellow that matches the device").
- **Type:** black or white only, chosen by computed contrast.
- **Buttons:** an outline pill or a single inverted fill.
- **Nothing else:** no second accent and no gradient.
- Sections change by switching to the next flat colour band, not by adding rules.

### Monochrome gallery (expressive)
*Rests on at least 143 "no accent" sites, nearly all expressive.*
- **Ground:** white, warm off-white or pure black.
- **Ink:** near-black or off-white. Grey is the second level for captions, client names and inactive nav.
- **No interface hue:** no accent and no coloured buttons. The current nav item is shown by full ink against grey, or by an underline.
- **Work:** images and screenshots carry all the colour, set edge to edge or with small gutters.

### Photo hero with a scrim (all characters)
*Rests on 369 image-ground sites: 285 expressive, 69 humanistic, 15 technical.*
- **Image:** a full-bleed photo or video, darkened by an overlay or a bottom gradient scrim.
- **Type:** white or cream, placed low-left where the scrim is darkest.
- **Accent:** one small call to action, as a white pill or an outline pill.
- **Notes:** the photo's own palette often sets the accent ("burnt orange CTA echoes the sofa colour in the photo").
- **Never** put type on an undimmed bright photo. That is where the low-contrast failures cluster.
