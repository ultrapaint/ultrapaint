# Page copy

A public page's words: its headline, subhead and eyebrow, its actions, its proof, its prices, and
the copy shape of each kind of page.

## The headline

About `5` words: about `6` for a technical product, `4` for an expressive site, where the name
alone often stands. A claim, not a label; short stacked sentences with full stops work in every
character ("Send it. Win it.", "Real food. Honest guides. No fluff."), and section titles may
take the full stop too ("How we compare."). A two-tone headline, the claim then its continuation
in muted at the same size, often replaces the subhead.

## The subhead

One sentence, about `15` words (`19` technical, `13` expressive), written only when it adds what
the headline cannot: who it is for, the price, how it works. Otherwise the headline stands alone.

## The eyebrow

None by default. One stays only when it says what the heading does not: news (a "New" chip, about
six words of announcement and an arrow), proof (a rating with its score and its source, only one
the product really has), a sequence ("01 · Agents research", "Step 4"), a card's category.

## Actions

Two, one heavier: a filled primary starting with a verb ("Get started", "Book a demo", "Download
for Mac", "Reserve"), beside an outline, a grey fill or a text link with an arrow ("See demo").
The header may repeat the hero's primary; nothing else on the first screen does. A portfolio or a
magazine has none: the work and the headlines are the content.

## Proof, once, beside the claim

Under the actions, the single strongest proof: stars and a review count, an avatar stack and a
number, or a row of greyscale logos with no "Trusted by" heading. One small muted line of
reassurance may sit under the button ("14-day trial · No credit card required").

## Numbers where the decision is made

Figures as the product counts them: "Hosted 373,571 people in 78,388 events". A price sits with
its action: in the subhead ("€29/month or €290/year"), in the button ("Buy a licence · €39", "Add
to bag – €66"), a sale price beside its struck-through original. A date sits on its card:
"Category · Date".

## Copy by kind of page

- **Technical hero**: the news eyebrow, a 6-word two-tone headline, a 19-word subhead saying what
  it does for whom (product nouns inline can stand for a feature list), the verb primary and a
  quiet secondary, one reassurance line, the logos. Exact, sometimes dry, never vague.
- **Pricing**: a headline of about `7` words and no subhead; the billing toggle with its saving
  beside it ("2 months free"); each plan its name, one line, its price with a small muted unit
  ("/mo", "per member per month") and one button; feature lists open with "Everything in Pro,
  plus:" and bold their quantities ("**10,000** API calls").
- **Shop**: a headline of `2–6` words, a subhead of `0–6`; a card's name (a pun when the brand
  jokes, a plain type line under it), its price, one badge only when it says something ("Best
  seller"); trust claims as one line ("Free shipping · No lock-ins"); an offer modal states one
  offer and has one field.
- **Restaurant or hotel**: the name is the headline, no subhead, one action ("Book a table"); the
  content is hours, address, phone and menu.
- **Title screen**: the title, `1–4` words; one one-word action ("Play", "Enter"); where sound
  matters, one line ("Best experienced with sound"); an escape ("Skip intro") as the only second
  action.
- **Statement**: one paragraph of `17–35` words (up to `72`) is the headline, with no subhead, no
  eyebrow and at most one quiet text link ("Let's chat").
- **Portfolio**: the name and a one-sentence bio stand for the headline; labels are plain words
  ("Info", "Work", "Email"); the work carries a client and a year, no more.
- **Editorial**: every story a kicker (its category), a headline of `8–12` words, a one-line dek
  and a byline after "By".
- **Help centre**: a headline of `2–6` words, often a question, no subhead; search is the hero.

## Check

| You see | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| A subhead that restates the headline | Delete it |
| An eyebrow naming what its heading names | Delete it; keep news, proof, a number or a category |
| Two actions of equal weight | One filled with a verb; the other outline, grey or a link |
| Proof stated twice (a bar and an eyebrow; stars, laurels and logos) | The strongest one, under the actions |
| A price far from its button | In the button or the sentence beside it |
| A plan's features repeating the plan before it | "Everything in Pro, plus:" |
| A template token, a broken glyph or a fallback face in the copy | Real copy in the real face, checked on screen |
| A portfolio or magazine with a "Get started" button | No action |
