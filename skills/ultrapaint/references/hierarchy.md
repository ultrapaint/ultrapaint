# Hierarchy

Every screen has one thing it is for. Make that the first thing the eye lands on, and rank
everything else below it.

Name the screen's purpose and its primary action in one sentence before placing a node. When you
cannot, the brief is unclear: ask the user.

## One primary action

One action per screen takes the primary variant. The others take the secondary, outline or ghost
variants by how often they are used. A screen whose job is reading, a dashboard, may have none. A
destructive action sits apart from the primary, at the far end or in a menu. Past three secondary
actions, the rest go behind a "More" menu.

## The squint test

Look at the screenshot as if squinting: the content the screen is for and its primary action are
the two heaviest shapes. When five things compete for first place, demote four.

Rows of equal weight are a menu, not a hierarchy. When one choice is the likely one, it leads;
when none is, there are few of them.

## Rank with position, then weight and colour, then size

Importance goes to the top and the leading edge first. Then a heavier weight or the foreground
colour against muted. Size is the loudest tool and comes last. One tool per step of importance;
all three on one element shouts.

## Headings lead

The page title says what the screen is in two to four words: "Invoices", "Billing settings".
Every heading, the title among them, is the first thing in its section, and the text below it
adds what the heading does not say: the organisation, the place, the category. What frames the
whole page sits in its header.

## Order follows use

What the user reads first goes first; what they do most goes where the hand already is. In a row,
the identifying content leads and metadata and actions trail. The one number the user came for
sits at the top of its group, larger and heavier than its label; a small muted label may sit above
it. A price is a large figure at regular weight beside a small muted unit; an old price sits above
it, struck through, muted, at about half its size. A featured item (the recommended plan) stands
out by its surface, inverted or filled; its action stays the primary button.

## Nesting stops at three levels

Past three containers deep, a heading and a gap say more than another box.

## Before you finish

| You see | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Two buttons in the primary variant on one screen | Keep the one the screen is for; the other becomes secondary |
| The title repeated in the first paragraph or a card heading | Cut the repeat |
| A small label above a heading ("Features", "School · Desio") | Cut it; what it adds goes below the heading or in the page header |
| A group of navigation items or settings rows under a bold heading | A small label: the group's name, quiet beside its rows, a step above their details (`references/typography.md`) |
| A list of choices all set alike, none leading | The likely one leads; the rest fewer, or behind search |
| The key figure below its metadata | Move it to the top of its group |
| A card in a card in a card | Replace the inner containers with a heading and a gap |
| Delete beside Save in the same style | Destructive variant, moved apart |
