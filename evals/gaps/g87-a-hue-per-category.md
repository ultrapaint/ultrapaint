**What happened.** Two designs gave every category its own colour where nothing needed one. The
changelog gave each of five entry types (new, improved, fixed, deprecated, breaking) a coloured
dot, run 2 again in a row of chips over the entries; only breaking needs attention. The calendar,
whose brief asked for five people's calendars "told apart without a rainbow", gave each person a
colour bar in run 2. The person chose run 1 on both, the quieter one, agreeing with the call that
gave these reasons.

**The skill now.** It asks for the rainbow. `references/color.md`, One hue, one meaning:
"Categories (tags, calendar events, the steps of a process) take their own hues, at least `30°`
from the primary and from each other", and its table: "Categories flattened to the accent and
grey | Each its own hue". `SKILL.md`: "categories take their own hues"; `character.md`
(humanistic): "categories in their own hues".

**Proposed.** A category is told by its label. A hue of its own goes to the one category a reader
must not miss (breaking, urgent, overdue), or, where a reader scans by category and no label is in
view (a week of several people's events), to tints of one hue or greys before separate hues. The
table row becomes "A hue for every category where a label tells them apart | The label, and colour
for the one that needs attention".

**Test.** The briefs with categories (`fresh-changelog`, `fresh-team-calendar`,
`fresh-sprint-board`, `fresh-support-inbox`, `fresh-inventory-table`), 2 runs each, Opus designing
with the candidate, against the skill on main: distinct category hues per page. It passes when no
page has more than two where the main runs have three or more, and the person prefers the
candidate's pages.
